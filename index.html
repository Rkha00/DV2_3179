Child development and learning in Australia

More Children Seen

More Australian children are being identified as autistic or with ADHD than ever before. Who are they, where do they live, and what support reaches them?

How common is it?

4.3%

of Australian children aged 5–14 were autistic in 2022, up from 2.8% in 2015.

In a class of 25 children, that is about one child in every classroom.

Autism is a lifelong difference in how a person communicates, interacts and experiences the world. ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) affects attention, activity and impulse control.

Awareness over time

Both autism and ADHD are being identified far more often than a decade ago. This mostly reflects better awareness and diagnosis, not necessarily more children with these conditions.

Who is diagnosed?

Boys are diagnosed far more often than girls, but the gap is closing as girls are recognised more often. Age matters too: some conditions are identified early, others later.

Where is support reaching?

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) funds support for children with a lasting disability or a delay in their development. How many children it reaches depends a lot on where they live.

What this means

More children are being recognised as autistic or with ADHD, and girls are increasingly part of that story. But support depends on where a child lives and what label they receive. Recognition is rising; making sure support keeps up is the next challenge.
